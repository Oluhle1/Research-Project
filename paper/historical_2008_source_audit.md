## Exact original quotations

### Notice and statutory basis

**Physical/printed page 3, GN R898:**

> SOCIAL ASSISTANCE ACT, 2004

> REGULATIONS RELATING TO THE APPLICATION FOR AND PAYMENT OF SOCIAL ASSISTANCE AND THE REQUIREMENTS OR CONDITIONS IN RESPECT OF ELIGIBILITY FOR SOCIAL ASSISTANCE

> The Minister of Social Development has, in terms of section 32 of the Social Assistance Act, 2004 (Act No. 13 of 2004), and with the concurrence of the Minister of Finance, made the regulations in the Schedule.

This is an issued regulation notice with its own commencement provision, rather than a call for comments on a draft.

### Repeal and temporary saving

**Physical/printed page 72, regulation 38(1):**

> The regulations published by Government Notice R.418 in Government Gazette 18771 of 31 March 1998, Government Notice R.813 in Government Gazette 20235 of 25 June 1999, Government Notice R.1233 in Government Gazette 22852 of 23 November 2001, Government Notice R.460 in Government Gazette 24630 of 31 March 2003, and Government Notice R162 in Government Gazette 27316 of 22 February 2005 are hereby repealed;

**Physical/printed page 73, regulation 38(2):**

> Despite regulation 38(1) of these regulations, regulations 4(4)(a) and 32(2)(b) of Regulation 8156 published in Government Notice R.162 in Government Gazette 27316 of 22 February 2005 remain in force until 31 December 2008.

The wording **“Regulation 8156”** is retained as printed. This limited saving expires in 2008; it is not evidence of a later formula amendment.

### Commencement

**Physical/printed page 73, regulation 39:**

> These Regulations come into effect on the date of publication of this notice.

Together with the notice/Gazette date, this establishes commencement on **22 August 2008**.

### Formula, annual units and maximum

**Physical/printed page 74, Annexure A(1):**

> The maximum amount of an older person's grant, disability grant or war veteran's grant shall from time to time be determined by the Minister with the concurrence of the Minister of Finance by notice in the Gazette.

This requires the applicable rate notice; the original base does not itself verify the later R1,350/R1,410 monthly rates.

**Physical/printed page 74, Annexure A(2):**

> The formula for the determination of the value of the older persons grant, disability grant and war veterans grant to be paid to an applicant is -

> D = 1,3A − 0,5B

**Physical/printed pages 74–75, Annexure A(2)(i)–(iii):**

> (i) A = the maximum social grant payable per annum as approved;

> (ii) B = the annual income of the applicant in accordance with regulation 19, which shall include the income from assets as determined in regulation 19(3), after permissible deductions contemplated in regulation 20 are effected;

> (iii) D = annual social grant amount payable, which must not exceed the amount equal to A.

The full original B definition includes income from assets and permissible deductions. An annual private-income proxy is a research operationalisation rather than a reproduction of the entire legal means calculation.

### Desertion provisions and minimum payment

**Physical/printed page 75, Annexure A(3):**

> Where a person in a spousal relationship has been deserted by his or her spouse for a continuous period of at least three months and the spouse is unwilling to support that person, the applicant is to be regarded as not being in a spousal relationship for the purpose of determining the means as contemplated in regulation 19.

**Same page, Annexure A(4):**

> The Agency must, if it is convinced that a person in a spousal relationship was deserted by his or her spouse for a continuous period of at least three months, and the spouse is unwilling to support that person, grant approval to disregard the means of the spouse.

**Same page, Annexure A(5):**

> No grant amounting to less than R100 per month is payable.

The exception has stated factual and Agency-approval requirements. Survey categories such as separated or nonresident spouse do not, by themselves, establish those requirements. This quotation is evidence of the 2008 wording, with continuity into 2014–2015 still requiring the relevant amendment review.

### Original asset criterion

**Physical/printed page 76, Annexure A(6):**

> No grant may be paid to any person not in a spousal relationship whose total assets as contemplated in regulation 19 exceeds 40 times A or to any person in a spousal relationship whose total assets exceeds 80 times A.

These are assets, not the study's annual income ceilings. The later 2011 notice expressly substitutes Annexure A(6), so quoting this original asset criterion as unchanged through the survey would be incorrect.

## Regulation 1: spouse

Page 15, exact text:

> “spouse” means a person who is the spouse or partner of a person in accordance with the Marriage Act, 1961 (Act No. 25 of 1961), the Recognition of Customary Marriages Act, 1998 (Act No. 120 of 1998) or the Civil Union Act 2006 (Act No. 17 of 2006) or the tenets of any Asiatic religion and “spousal relationship” has a corresponding meaning;

The final religious terminology is reproduced as printed. “Partner” is linked to the listed legal/religious bases; the definition does not independently classify every survey “living together” response as a legal spousal relationship. This original wording supplies direct 2008 evidence, while its precise later application still requires the amendment history.

## Regulation 18: financial criteria, joint income and desertion

Page 40, paragraph (1), exact text:

> The financial criteria in terms of which applicants for an older person’s grant, a disability grant, a war veteran’s grant, a child support grant, a foster child grant and a care dependency grant, respectively, qualify are set out in Annexures A, B, C and D, respectively.

Paragraph (2), exact text:

> The income of an applicant and his or her spouse must be taken into account irrespective of whether the couple is married in or out of community of property under the Marriage Act, 1961 or any provisions contained in an ante-nuptial contract.

Paragraph (3), exact text:

> The Agency must, if it is satisfied that an applicant has been deserted by his or her spouse for a continuous period of at least three months, and the spouse is unwilling to support the applicant, grant approval for the means of that spouse not to be taken into consideration when determining the means of the applicant.

The image reads **“determining”**; OCR’s `deterrnining` is not reproduced. The exception requires continuous desertion for at least three months **and** unwillingness to support, with Agency satisfaction and approval. A survey response of “separated” alone does not establish these conditions. Residence and reciprocal household person-number links used by the study are research consistency requirements, not conditions stated in these quoted clauses.

## Regulation 19(1): annual and half-joint income

Page 41, exact introductory text:

> For the purposes of determining means, in respect of social assistance, except for a grant in-aid and the foster child grant, the income of the applicant is deemed to be the annual income for an applicant not in a spousal relationship, or half the annual income of the applicant and his or her spouse, where the applicant is in a spousal relationship, and “income” means—

This authenticates annual units and half the combined annual income in the original 2008 text. It does not establish an unchanged 2014–2015 rule. The clause concerns income; it does not halve every asset value or replace the separately prescribed asset criteria.

Exact income classes, with clauses that continue across pages joined:

Page 41, paragraphs (a)–(c):

> a) any compensation payable to an applicant or his or her spouse or dependent child in cash or otherwise;

> b) any profits, withdrawals or other benefits derived from a business concern or farm of which the applicant or his or her spouse or dependent child is the owner or holds property rights in respect thereof;

> c) any payment which an applicant or his or her spouse or dependent child receives from a trust or inheritance or as an employee;

Pages 41–42, paragraph (d):

> d) any payment which an applicant or his or her spouse or dependent child derives from property rights;

Page 42, paragraphs (e)–(i):

> e) any pension or annuity payable to an applicant or his or her spouse or dependent child in terms of the provisions of a pension or provident or retirement annuity fund established in terms of any Act, but excluding benefits received in terms of the Act;

> f) any ex gratia amount received in cash or otherwise by an applicant or his or her spouse or dependent child;

> g) any rental payable to an applicant or his or her spouse or dependent child for providing accommodation;

> h) any profits, withdrawals or other benefits derived from farming activities where fixed property is rented by an applicant or his or her spouse or dependent child;

> i) any maintenance received from a person obliged in law to provide such maintenance;

Pages 42–43, paragraph (j):

> j) any other income, not mentioned in these regulations including interest and dividends, generated from assets of an applicant or his or her spouse or dependent child; and

Page 43, paragraph (k):

> k) any income or financial support derived from a South African or international organisation, excluding social assistance.

The repeated words **“or dependent child”** and the separate **ex gratia** category are visible in the original. They must not be omitted when quoting the 2008 income definition or treating its list as equivalent to the later 2022 list.

## Regulation 19: relinquished income and assets

Page 43, paragraph (2)(b), exact text:

> Despite the provisions of paragraph (a) the income contemplated in paragraph (a) must not be taken into account after a period of five years has lapsed from the date of donation or relinquishment.

Page 45, paragraph (4), exact text:

> Despite subregulation (3) the value of immovable property owned and occupied by the applicant and his or her spouse must not be taken into account when regard is had to the assets of the applicant and his or her spouse, in the case of an older person’s grant, a disability grant and a war veteran’s grant.

Page 45, paragraph (5), exact text:

> The Agency must, if it is of the opinion that an applicant or his or her spouse impoverished himself or herself or relinquished assets to obtain a social grant, take such assets into account, which may include any property or asset donated, transferred or sold below market value by either the applicant or his or her spouse to a connected party.

The original paragraph (5) ends at **“to a connected party.”** It contains no five-year proviso. Do not transfer paragraph (2)(b)’s income limitation to this asset clause or retrospectively import the 2022 asset proviso. This audit does not reproduce the complete asset list in paragraphs (3)(a)–(d), pages 43–45.

## Regulation 20: deductions

Page 45, introductory text:

> When determining the income of an applicant and his or her spouse in the case of a social grant, the Agency must, on submission of acceptable documentary proof, allow the following deductions or contributions—

Page 46, exact deduction classes:

> a) current obligatory contributions of an employee to a pension, provident or retirement annuity fund established in terms of an Act or, in the absence of such an obligatory contribution, another contribution which does not exceed 22 percent of the net income of the applicant;

> b) current deductions in respect of an employee’s tax or standard income tax;

> c) current membership fees to an approved medical scheme in the Republic, established in terms of the Medical Schemes Act 1998 (Act No. 131 of 1998), paid from income generated from services rendered or income from any other source; and

> d) current contributions to the unemployment insurance fund paid from income generated from services rendered.

Acceptable documentary proof is an express condition. The 22% limit attaches to the alternative contribution in the absence of an obligatory contribution, rather than imposing a universal ceiling on every obligatory contribution.

## Historical conclusions and remaining limits

The correct 2008 base has now been supplied and its audited means/spouse provisions directly read. Earlier statements that its full text was unavailable are historical records of the previous audit stage, not the current source position. The earlier `415.pdf` remains a different Gazette (31342) and supplies no substitute for this original.

This closes the source gap for the **original 2008 wording** of the clauses quoted here. It does not establish that their wording was unchanged in October 2014–October 2015. The known 2011 amendment and later 2022 replacement must be placed within the complete relevant amendment chain; matching endpoint clauses alone cannot prove continuity. The visibly different income categories and asset proviso reinforce the need to identify the timing and wording of intervening amendments.

The correct base alone does not authenticate the original 2014/2015 maximum-rate notices or an intervening amendment establishing the survey-period grant formula/payment boundary. Those remain separate primary-source requirements. Formula, Annexure A and commencement findings should be taken from the companion chronology audit rather than inferred from the means clauses reproduced here.

The study’s resource measure, resident/reciprocal spouse links and ambiguous-status treatment remain research assumptions until their period-specific legal premises are authenticated. A survey category does not establish legal spouse status, an income-only screen does not apply the entire means test, and the desertion exception requires facts and Agency action not supplied by a simple separation response. No research threshold, classification, result or model was changed on the basis of this source review.

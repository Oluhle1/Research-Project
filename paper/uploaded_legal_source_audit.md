## Exact spouse and means definitions

Regulation 1 (Gazette pp. 10–11; PDF pp. 8–9):

> “spouse” means a person who is the spouse or partner of a person in accordance with the Marriage Act, 1961 (Act No. 25 of 1961), the Recognition of Customary Marriages Act, 1998 (Act No. 120 of 1998) or the Civil Union Act, 2006 (Act No. 17 of 2006) or the tenets of any Asiatic religion and “spousal relationship” has a corresponding meaning;

The historical term in the final limb is transcribed as printed. The definition does not contain an independent rule that all people who report “living together” in a survey are legal spouses. Its word “partner” is expressly linked to the listed legal/religious bases. This 2022 definition cannot authenticate a 2014–2015 legal spouse definition or resolve the survey partnership categories for that period.

Regulation 1 (Gazette p. 9; PDF p. 7):

> “means” for the purposes of the older persons grant, disability grant, war veterans grant and social relief of distress, excluding social relief of distress as contemplated in regulation 11(2), means the income and assets of—(a) an applicant; or (b) an applicant and his or her spouse;

## Regulations 20–21: joint means and desertion

Regulation 20(1) refers the grant-specific financial criteria to Annexures A–D. The relevant older-persons, disability and war-veterans grant criteria are in Annexure A.

Regulation 20(2) (Gazette p. 25; PDF p. 23):

> The income of an applicant and his or her spouse must be taken into account irrespective of whether the couple is married in or out of community of property under the Marriage Act, 1961 or any provisions contained in an ante-nuptial contract.

Regulation 20(3) (same page):

> The Agency must, if it is satisfied that an applicant has been deserted by his or her spouse for a continuous period of at least three months, and the spouse is unwilling to support the applicant, grant approval for the means of that spouse not to be taken into consideration when determining the means of the applicant.

This is a combined condition: continuous desertion for at least three months **and** unwillingness to support, with Agency satisfaction and approval. A survey response of “separated” alone does not establish those facts or the approval.

Regulation 21(1), introductory text (Gazette p. 25; PDF p. 23):

> For the purposes of determining means, in respect of social assistance, except for a grant in-aid and the foster child grant, the income of the applicant is deemed to be the annual income for an applicant not in a spousal relationship, or half the annual income of the applicant and his or her spouse, where the applicant is in a spousal relationship, and ‘income’ means—

The 2022 income rule is therefore the applicant’s annual income for a person outside a spousal relationship, or **half the combined annual income** for an applicant in a spousal relationship. This is a rule for income. The asset provisions separately refer to the applicant’s/spouse’s assets and Annexure A sets separate single/couple asset limits; do not silently infer that all assets are halved by regulation 21(1).

The following is a concise description of the regulation 21(1)(a)–(j) categories, not a verbatim substitute for those paragraphs: compensation in cash or otherwise; benefits/profits/withdrawals from an owned business or farm; trust, inheritance or employment payments; property-right payments; pensions/annuities with the stated social-assistance exclusion; accommodation rent; benefits from farming on rented fixed property; maintenance; other income including interest and dividends generated from assets; and organisational income or financial support subject to the social-assistance exclusion.

Particularly relevant exact exclusions/inclusions (Gazette p. 26; PDF p. 24):

> (e) any pension or annuity payable to an applicant or his or her spouse in terms of the provisions of a pension or provident or retirement annuity fund established in terms of any Act, but excluding benefits received in terms of the Act;

> (h) any maintenance received from a person obliged in law to provide such maintenance;

> (i) any other income, not mentioned in these Regulations including interest and dividends, generated from assets of an applicant or his or her spouse; and

> (j) any income or financial support derived from a South African or international organisation, excluding social assistance.

Regulation 21(2) provides for donated/relinquished income used to obtain a grant, with a five-year limit. Regulation 21(3) lists relevant assets; regulation 21(4) excludes immovable property owned and occupied by the applicant and spouse from these asset calculations; regulation 21(5) addresses assets relinquished to obtain a grant, also with a five-year limit. These are additional reasons that a survey income-only screen cannot prove full legal eligibility.

## Regulation 22: deductions

Regulation 22 (Gazette p. 27; PDF p. 25), exact text:

> When determining the income of an applicant and his or her spouse in the case of a social grant, the Agency must, on submission of acceptable documentary proof, allow the following deductions or contributions:
>
> (a) Current obligatory contributions of an employee to a pension, provident or retirement annuity fund established in terms of an Act or, in the absence of such an obligatory contribution, another contribution which does not exceed 22 per cent of the net income of the applicant;
>
> (b) current deductions in respect of an employee’s tax or standard income tax;
>
> (c) current membership fees to an approved medical scheme in the Republic, established in terms of the Medical Schemes Act 1998 (Act 131 of 1998), paid from income generated from services rendered or income from any other source; and
>
> (d) current contributions to the unemployment insurance fund paid from income generated from services rendered.

The documentary-proof condition is part of the rule. The 22% limit in paragraph (a) attaches to the alternative contribution when there is no obligatory contribution; do not describe it as an across-the-board cap on every obligatory pension contribution.

## Annexure A: formula, minimum and assets

Annexure A (Gazette p. 40; PDF p. 38), paragraph (1), states that the Minister with the concurrence of the Minister of Finance determines the maximum grant amount by notice in the Gazette. It does **not** state the historical R1,350/R1,410 monthly maxima used by the research screen.

Paragraph (2), exact text:

> The formula for the determination of the value of the older persons’ grant, disability grant and war veterans’ grant to be paid to an applicant is—
>
> D = 1,6A - 0,4B
>
> where—
>
> (i) A = the maximum social grant payable per annum as approved;
>
> (ii) B = the annual income of the applicant in accordance with regulation 19, which shall include the income from assets as determined in regulation 21(3), after permissible deductions contemplated in regulation 22 are effected; and
>
> (iii) D = annual social grant amount payable, which must not exceed the amount equal to A.

The original image explicitly prints decimal **1,6**, not 1,4. A, B and D are annual quantities. The reference to **regulation 19** in paragraph (2)(ii) is also in the original image; regulation 19 in this 2022 document concerns the date of an application for social relief of distress. The reference to regulation 21(3) is printed as quoted although that provision lists assets. These apparent cross-reference problems must not be silently repaired when quoting the notice.

Paragraph (3):

> Where a person in a spousal relationship has been deserted by his or her spouse for a continuous period of at least three months and the spouse is unwilling to support that person, the applicant is to be regarded as not being in a spousal relationship for the purpose of determining the means as contemplated in regulation 21.

Paragraph (4):

> The Agency must, if it is convinced that a person in a spousal relationship was deserted by his or her spouse for a continuous period of at least three months, and the spouse is unwilling to support that person, grant approval to disregard the means of the spouse.

Paragraph (5):

> No grant amounting to less than R100 per month is payable.

This confirms a R100 monthly minimum **payable amount** in 2022, equivalent to R1,200 annually. It is not a rule to round every lower calculated amount up to R100. It does not establish the same payment boundary for 2014–2015.

Paragraph (6):

> No grant may be paid to any person not in a spousal relationship whose total assets as contemplated in regulation 21 exceeds 55 times A or to any person in a spousal relationship whose total assets exceeds 110 times [A].

The brackets around the final `[A]` are printed in the source. This is additional eligibility screening distinct from the income formula.
